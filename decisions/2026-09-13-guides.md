# Decision: guides instead of collections

Planned 12 and 13 September 2026, built from 17 September. The owner redirected a proposal in
`decisions/2026-09-11-competitor-gap-analysis.md` and made the product calls below; the
implementation followed a plan kept outside the repository and summarised here.

---

## What changed, and why

**Collections were section 5 of the gap analysis**: a named, ordered list of published builds
with an intro and a note on each, deferred until notes on picks and `@` mentions existed. Both
shipped in `6bf2695`.

**The owner redirected it on 12 September.** A list of builds adds little over the All shelf
and its filters, which is the argument that retired the curated shelf on 8 September, one scope
down. What is worth building is a **guide**: sectioned prose with builds named inside it.

**The reason to build it at all is run state.** A build named in a guide renders with its live
verdict, from the same `verdictForShown` the run screen uses over the library. So a guide reads
differently at the fourth Exit than at the Crossroads, which no competitor can do, because none
of them has a run. Without that it would be a wiki article and should not be built.

**This reverses two verdicts of 11 September**: "no guide type" and "no sectioned long-form
editor". Both were right for the question they answered, which was whether to copy Mobalytics'
guide format. A guide that reads the run is a different thing.

## What the owner decided

1. Guides, not collections. The builds in a guide are whatever its build mentions name, in the
   order they appear. Nobody maintains a second list
2. Live: a build mention shows its verdict during a run, and a guide can be opened mid run
3. Sectioned, with a few prompted fields
4. A note about somebody else's build appears inside the guide only, never on that build's
   listing or card
5. Two menu entries, Builds and Guides, in the rows the build manager and the exchange held, so
   the menu does not grow. Each screen switches between your side and everybody's
6. The switch is a two-way control in the heading row. No bars on the window edges. Characters
   stay decoration and are never the control
7. A guide card shows its most mentioned things, derived from the mentions, typed by nobody
8. A seed guide, "How to beat the RNG", with simple rules the owner fills in
9. Charon on everybody's side of Builds and of Guides, Schelemeus on yours, Odysseus for the
   Wiki, Dora keeps the tour and the rooms under construction. Utility pages get no figure

## The moderation call, 17 September

A listed guide carries free text a stranger wrote, which is the first of the conditions
`REQUIREMENTS.md` 5 named on 8 September as changing its answer. Asked to choose between listed
with a report route and a hide, listed without them, and unlisted, the owner chose **listed,
with both, in the same change**. `REQUIREMENTS.md` 5 records it as a reversal for guides only.

How it is built, in `worker/guides.ts`: a report is one row per person per guide and is never
returned; a hide is a column that no route writes, set by a moderator with a statement against
the database; a hidden guide leaves every list and answers only its author. Nothing hides on a
count of reports.

## The likes call, 17 September

Saves were in from the start. Reads were ruled out in the plan: a database write on every view
by anybody, which `run_worker_first` exists to avoid, and the easiest number there is to
inflate. Asked whether guides should also have likes, a second popularity number beside saves,
the owner chose **saves and likes**. Both are counts per guide, both refuse the author's own,
and neither says who.

## The sections call, 18 September

The first proposal was a set of prompts shaped like a run: the opening, the middle Regions, the
Guardian. The owner turned it down because **a guide does not have to be a run**. It can be how
to play one weapon, how to beat one boss, how to unlock one achievement, how to make the
stupidest build that still works, or how to run at maximum Fear.

What was chosen is four prompts that name no part of a run, and sections of the author's own
past them, **up to eight in all**:

1. What this is (and who it is for)
2. What you need (unlocks, Arcana, weapon, Fear, anything assumed)
3. How it goes (the guide itself)
4. What to watch for (mistakes, and what to do when it goes wrong)

An empty prompt is skipped when the guide is read.

**One implementation call on top of that, and easy to reverse.** The words before the brackets
are the heading a reader sees, and the words inside them are a hint shown beside the field in
the editor and never published. Every word the owner approved is on screen where the author
writes; a reader sees "What you need" rather than the whole prompt on every guide there is.
`PROMPTS` in `src/state/guides.ts` is the one place to change it, and a published guide keeps
the headings it was written under, so changing it later rewrites nothing already up.

**Item 9 read one way.** It names Charon for everybody's side of both screens and Schelemeus for
"yours". He stands on your side of Guides as well as of Builds, which is the parallel reading.

## The seed guide's names, 25 September

Asked what "the upgrade token" and "the Hermes shrine" are called on screen, the owner said
**Pom of Power**, and **the Shrine of Hermes, the Surface shop, not Charon's**. Nothing in the
game is called a token; the other candidates offered were Rarify, a keepsake's charge to raise
one Boon's rarity, and the Daedalus Hammer.

The owner added a recollection: Charon's timed buffs, such as the rarity bonus on the Boons that
follow, are not sold at the Shrine of Hermes. **The scripts agree and go further.** The buffs
are `StoreData.RoomShop.Traits`, which only the Well of Charon sells from, and every Surface
Region sets `WellShopSpawnChance = 0.0`, so they cannot be bought on the Surface at all.
`guides/how-to-beat-the-rng.md` carries the sources.

The owner first chose to write both rules themselves, then the same day asked for the whole
guide written for them, **with other people's guides online as inspiration**, and said they
would read it and comment afterwards. Those guides were used as leads and every claim was
checked against the scripts before it went in. Two leads were dropped: that Athena's and
Dionysus's Boons scale well with Poms, when in Hades II no Encounter god's Boon can take a Pom
at all, which reads like Hades bleeding across; and a claim about levels matching rarities,
which nothing here needed. The checking also corrected two things this project had written the
day before, both now fixed in `CLAUDE.md`: a Mystery Boon does spend a keepsake, and the
Shrine's wait counts Encounters.

It is **a file in the repository, not a draft in the app**, because the app's only way to hand
somebody a draft is an import, and an import replaces everything the browser holds.
`src/data/seed-guide.test.ts` reads the file the way the editor will.

## Still open

- **Publishing the seed guide**, which only the owner's account can do, and their comments on
  it once they have read it
- **The switches' labels** are placeholders: Yours and The exchange on Builds, Yours and
  Everybody on Guides
- **A cap on mentions in a build's own long fields.** Measured on the way: the 2000 character
  guard on a build's link only ever held for How it works and If the run goes your way written
  as words, and eight mentions at the front of them reach 2022 with boons and 2035 with builds
